## Outcome

carries a visible "Civica calculation" marker (`publisher-attribution/v1`).

- **Freedom House row (owner decision a).** The value reads "Free · Freedom in
  the World 2024" (or Partly Free / Not Free) and prints no number. An (i)
  note, also present as screen-reader text, says: "Freedom in the World 2024
  covers calendar year 2023. Civica's frozen release stored Freedom House's
  political rights and civil liberties ratings but not its status column, so
  Civica applied Freedom House's published rule to those ratings: an average
  rating of 1.0 to 2.5 is Free, 3.0 to 5.0 is Partly Free, and 5.5 to 7.0 is
  Not Free." The row's SourceDot names "Freedom in the World 2024, covering
  calendar year 2023" and the retrieval time recorded in the release's input

- **V-Dem row (owner decision b).** V-Dem's own Liberal Democracy Index figure
  at V-Dem's published precision (0.769 for Uruguay), with no Civica rank.

  runs inside `validate:claims-docs`, so it runs in every build, in the CI

## Audit facts (as found on 2026-09-29)

Production rendered Uruguay's Rankings section as (curl of
`/country/uruguay/civica-data?section=rankings`):

| Score | Value | Global rank | Trend | As of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V-Dem Liberal Democracy [Frozen release · 2024 Q4] | 0.77 (as of 2024-Q4) | 17 / 170 | — | 2024-Q4 |
| Freedom House Status [Frozen release · 2024 Q4] | Free (100/100) (as of 2024-Q4) | — | — | 2024-Q4 |

Both row SourceDots read "source timestamp: Unknown timestamp; upstream
vintage: Not supplied on this surface", and the Sources strip listed "V-Dem
2026-07-15" and "Freedom House 2026-07-11", sync dates later than the release
capture.

- The "100/100" was Civica's fixed-bound rescale of Freedom House's political
  rights plus civil liberties sum, ((14 − sum) ÷ 12) × 100 (`normalize-v2.ts`).
  41 of 190 release rows read 100 (sum 2). Freedom House's own Total Score for
  the same edition reaches 100 for one country (Finland; Uruguay's is 96). The
  status word came from Civica's rule on the sum, not Freedom House's Status
  column.
- "Global rank 17 / 170" was Civica's rank of Uruguay among the 170 V-Dem rows
  in the release, beside a Governance Evidence banner saying Civica does not
  rank the country.
- The legacy `country_metrics` rows had no recorded edition or retrieval time;
  Peru's 2023 CPI read 36 where Transparency International's published figure,
  retained in Civica's own series, is 33.

**Why no gate caught it.** The validators checked composite names, grade
words, SourceDot presence, protected-file bytes, and release pinning. None
compared a number shown next to a publisher's name with that publisher's own
figure.

## Freedom House status rule

Freedom House's FAQ states that the average of the two ratings determined the
status, and that the formula converting scores into status is unchanged since
the 2020 edition; the FIW 2019 methodology gives the bands (Free 1.0–2.5,
Partly Free 3.0–5.0, Not Free 5.5–7.0). Checked on 2026-09-29 against Freedom
House's FIW 2024 country pages at every rule boundary:

| Stored sum | Rule | Freedom House page |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Free | Colombia: Free (70); Brazil: Free (72) |
| 6 | Partly Free | India: Partly Free (66); Ecuador: Partly Free (67) |
| 10 | Partly Free | Niger: Partly Free (33); Pakistan: Partly Free (35) |
| 11 | Not Free | Turkey: Not Free (33) |

Niger and Turkey share a Total Score of 33 yet differ in status, which
confirms that status follows the ratings, not the Total Score. The captured
workbook contains Freedom House's own Status column; the release adapter kept
only the ratings, so Civica applies the rule (follow-up F7 captures the column
at the next capture). The live check's rule comparison proves that two
retained copies of the ratings agree; it cannot prove the status equals
Freedom House's column.

## Four clocks

Each Rankings row keeps four clocks apart (APR-D058): observation year (2024
for V-Dem; 2023 for Freedom in the World 2024), publisher edition (V-Dem
Country-Year Core v15; Freedom in the World 2024), retrieval time (the
2026-07-10T20:56:16.125Z capture-record time that the release's hash-bound
input manifest records for all four inputs), and the Civica release
(`ci-beta-r5-2024-Q4`). The release rows' `temporal_coverage` "2024" is the
Freedom in the World edition year and is not read for display.
`publisher-scores.test.ts` proves every clock against the input manifest, whose
SHA-256 is the release contract's `inputManifestSha256`.

## The permanent check

1. **Typed origin.** Score rows, the scores API, and the Conditions contract
   carry a `DisplayedValueOrigin` (`publisher_published`,
   `publisher_rule_applied`, or `civica_calculation`), rendered only through
   `ValueOriginNote`.
2. **Unit and contract tests** (in `npm test`): the rule for every sum from 2
   to 14, the shapers, the former "Free (100/100)" row failing under every
   origin claim, the clocks against the manifest, rendered markup for the
   Rankings table and the Conditions panel, compare, and explorer, the API
   example replay, and the scan with its seeded mutations.
3. **Static scan** (`validate:publisher-attribution`, DB-free, fails closed).
   Code is compared with comments removed. It checks 17 registered surfaces
   and their required disclosures (4 are exceptions tied to follow-ups F1–F4),
   classifies all 37 files that render a `SourceDot` or `FactValueDot` (a new
   one fails until classified), lists all 50 readers of Civica-derived fields
   (`normalizedScore`, rank fields, composite tables, `country_metrics`) with
   the tokens each may use, keeps the Conditions formulas equal to the frozen
   parameter contract, and ratchets exceptions and reader allowances against
   `scripts/publisher-attribution-baseline.json`: a new one needs an approving
   decision or task and a deliberate `--update-baseline`. Before scanning, it
   proves itself: the value check must reject the former row and accept the
   current ones, and eleven seeded mutations must each introduce their own
   rule's failure.
4. **Live check** (`validate:publisher-attribution:live`, read-only
   `REPEATABLE READ` transaction). Results in
   `publisher-attribution-live-2026-09-29.json`: all 169 V-Dem rows with a
   retained exact copy match it at three decimals (Taiwan has no retained
   copy); all 189 joinable Freedom House statuses equal the rule on the
   retained ratings; the former formatter's text fails for all 190 rows
   (Uruguay "Free (100/100)", Brazil "Free (75/100)", Hungary "Partly Free
   (67/100)", China "Not Free (8/100)"); 16 sample countries carry only the v2
   keys and manifest clocks, and their 31 API rows are withheld for rights;
   all 101 scored Conditions positions carry a registered `scoreOrigin` and
   replay. The one failure matches the registered exception below.

`validate:publisher-attribution` is a child of `validate:claims-docs`, so it
runs in `build:core` (every build, including Vercel), in the CI `verify` job
through `npm run build:ci`, and in Index change control. It runs no test
files and opens no database connection, because that gate passes the build's
environment to its children; the tests run in the gate's credential-free
`npm test` child. `BUILD_CORE_SHA256` and `.github/workflows/ci.yml` are
unchanged. The live check runs manually where `DATABASE_URL` is injected, like
every other `:live` validator.

## New finding: CLM-021

The live check found that all 50 Conditions Human Development values labelled
reference year 2023 that have a 2022 counterpart in the same retained series
equal it: India 0.644, Japan 0.920, and the United States 0.927 are UNDP's
Human Development Report 2023/24 figures for 2022. `indicator_history` holds
a different, unnamed UNDP series (India 0.685 for 2023, reported by the
design review as Human Development Report 2025 figures), so it cannot decide
the edition. Correcting the label needs a new immutable Conditions release, a
production write that needs the owner's authority and edition choice. CLM-021
(P0, open) tracks it, and the live exception
`conditions.hdi-reference-year` points to it; closing CLM-021 without removing
the exception fails the scan.

## Differences from the design and the review

- Decision ID APR-D174: APR-D173 was taken by DAT-037.
- Scope kept to the owner decisions: removing `democracyIndex` from two APIs,
  hardening the timestamp formatter, and labelling "Last synced" dates are
  follow-ups F6, F9, and F8, not part of this change.
- Conditions positions are hidden on `/compare`, as designed, because the
  page's existing banner promises source-native components; showing marked
  positions and rewriting the banner is a one-prop reversal if the owner
  prefers it.
- The scores API is rights-filtered with a strict zod contract rather than
  re-published unfiltered.
- Release identity on each row is the slim `{ releaseId, quarter,
  vintageLabel }`; the full identity carried a Freedom House coverage of "2024"
  that contradicted the row's observation year.
- The clustering signature records the original defect in the live report
  but decides nothing; the value comparisons are the checks.
- Two dead readers in `queries.ts` stay: `validate-conditions-components`
  pins one of them, and deleting them would not narrow the file's token
  allowance.
- The design kept the rule and shapers in `queries-scores.ts`. They moved to
  the new DB-free `src/lib/ci/publisher-scores.ts` because the
  `/design-system` demo uses the real Freedom House rule output and the
  cache-consistency gate forbids a static page from reaching a database
  module. The new file is registered as an Index-protected presentation file,
  so the rule stays under change control, and the golden test moved with it
  (`src/lib/ci/publisher-scores.test.ts`).

## Limitations

- The scan cannot see new arithmetic in a file that reads no registered
  field, such as a new percentage computed from raw seat counts. For that
  case the rule in `DESIGN.md` and `AGENTS.md` is a review rule.
- Reader allowances are per file. A new derived read inside an allowed
  multi-purpose file (`queries.ts`, `schemas.ts`, `shapes.ts`, `examples.ts`)
  is not caught.
- The live check compares with Civica's retained copies (the K1 v2 panel and
  the legacy HDI series), not with publisher websites. Global Peace Index and
  World Bank components have no second retained copy and are reported as
  unverified.
- Freedom House's own Status column is not captured; the rule's equivalence
  rests on Freedom House's published statement and the boundary checks above.
- Owner decisions still open: a public correction note for the former
  Freedom House value (F12; Major under `content/policies.md`), the
  "Rankings" section name (F10), and the CLM-021 edition choice. QA-013 visual
  baselines for the Civica Data tab, compare, the Conditions explorer, and
  `/design-system` need re-approval.
