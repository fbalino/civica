## 1. Draft selection criteria (GOV-008 prep)

This section is a draft input for GOV-008 ("Define reviewer roles and selection
criteria before naming people"). It proposes the shared framework plus
per-lane specifics that GOV-008's acceptance test requires (minimum expertise,
independence, conflicts, geographic/method diversity, deliverable, and
exclusion criteria). Nothing here is adopted policy until the owner and Codex
review it and it is folded into the actual GOV-008 artifact.

### 1.1 Shared framework — applies to every lane

**What "reviewer" means at this stage.** GOV-008/009 are pre-G4 preparation
(per `plan/10-academic-governance-review-and-outreach.md`: "Agent preparation
and quiet public reviewer research may precede G4; no reviewer solicitation
before G4"). Nobody on this longlist has been asked anything. The eventual
review packets these people would receive are already scoped in the checklist
— GOV-013 (atlas/data-curation packet), GOV-014 (Index tournament packet),
and GOV-015 (Pulse packet) — so criteria below are written against those
concrete deliverables, not an abstract "review our project" ask.

**Independence rule (tiered, not a blanket exclusion).** Per the owner's
explicit brief: affiliation with a Civica data source (V-Dem, Freedom House,
World Bank/WGI, Transparency International CPI, IPU Parline, Constitute
Project) is a *disclosure and conflict-management question*, not automatic
disqualification — in several lanes (especially Lane A) the strongest
methodologists are structurally likely to have worked with these projects at
some point, since they are the major players in the field. Proposed tiers:

- **Tier 1 — Disclose and manage (default, does not block invitation).**
  Person has published with, consulted for, or is affiliated with one of
  Civica's source projects, but their assigned review task does not require
  them to judge that source's own data quality (e.g., a V-Dem-affiliated
  measurement theorist reviewing Pulse's sampling design, not V-Dem's own
  scores). Disclose the relationship in the review record (GOV-018) and in
  any published disposition (GOV-020).
- **Tier 2 — Disclose and recuse from the specific judgment.** Person's
  affiliation directly overlaps the material under review (e.g., a current
  V-Dem core-team member asked to assess whether the Civica Index tournament
  correctly evaluated V-Dem-derived inputs). They can still review adjacent
  material (e.g., the Atlas provenance/reconciliation packet) with disclosure,
  but should not be the sole or deciding voice on the overlapping judgment —
  pair with a non-affiliated reviewer on that specific question.
- **Tier 3 — Exclude for this cycle.** Person has a financial relationship
  with Civica itself (paid consulting, equity, or compensation contingent on
  a favorable outcome — see GOV-012, which governs honoraria and explicitly
  bars outcome-contingent payment), is a named author/contributor to Civica
  content, or has an active, undisclosed commercial relationship with a
  directly competing product. This tier is about relationships to *Civica*,
  not to the *source organizations* Civica cites.

No person in this longlist has been contacted, so no conflict tier below is a
final determination — it is a flag for GOV-010's scoring rubric to weigh, and
the "Conflicts/dependencies" field on every candidate below exists so that
rubric has something to score against.

**Methodological and geographic diversity goals.** Applied per lane, not
just in aggregate, so no single lane collapses onto one school of thought or
one region:

- At least one candidate per core lane whose institutional base is outside
  the US and the EU/UK.
- At least one practitioner (data-curation professional, applied
  consultant, NGO/civil-society methodologist) alongside academic
  researchers in every lane where that distinction is meaningful (especially
  Lanes C, D, E).
- A mix of career stages — avoid a longlist that is entirely senior/famous
  names; mid-career, methods-focused researchers are favored because they
  are both more likely to have hands-on technical fit and more likely to
  have bandwidth to respond (this mirrors the "favor genuine methodological
  reviewers over famous names" instruction under which this research ran).
- Within Lane A specifically, deliberately include both scholars who work
  inside the major index projects (V-Dem, Freedom House-adjacent, WGI) and
  scholars who critique those projects from outside, so the eventual review
  isn't a closed loop of people validating their own ecosystem.

**Shared exclusion criteria (any one is disqualifying at longlist stage):**

1. No independently verifiable evidence of professional activity in the
   relevant field in roughly 2023–2026 (a dead or years-stale faculty page,
   no traceable recent output).
2. No public institutional/professional page to use as a contact channel —
   Civica does not collect personal emails or phone numbers, so someone
   reachable only through a personal channel cannot be added to this
   longlist at all (this is a data-collection constraint, not a judgment on
   the person).
3. A direct, currently active commercial relationship with a product that
   competes with Civica Atlas in a way that creates an obvious incentive
   conflict, without a plausible management path.
4. Prior undisclosed paid engagement with Civica (see Tier 3 above).
5. No verifiable scholarly, technical, or professional output in the
   specific lane — general prominence in an adjacent field is not sufficient
   (e.g., a well-known democracy pundit with no measurement-methodology
   publication record does not qualify for Lane A).

**Deliverable per lane (what the eventual bounded ask looks like).** This
previews GOV-011's "exact bounded review question" requirement so lane

everything":

### 1.2 Per-lane minimum expertise and lane-specific notes

**Lane A — Governance measurement / comparative-politics indices**
- Minimum expertise: peer-reviewed or working-paper record on measurement
  validity, latent-variable/aggregation methodology, or uncertainty
  quantification for at least one major cross-national governance/democracy
  index.
- Independence note: expect most strong candidates to have some V-Dem,
  Freedom House, WGI, or CPI touchpoint (Tier 1 by default); actively
  balance with outside critics.
- Exclusion addition: purely qualitative country-expertise scholars with no
  measurement-methodology contribution do not qualify for this lane
  specifically (they may fit a future country-content review, not this one).

**Lane B — Political event data**
- Minimum expertise: hands-on contribution to (building, coding, or
  methodologically critiquing) a political/conflict event dataset — coding
  reliability, media-selection-bias, or machine-vs-human coding comparisons.
- Independence note: Civica's Pulse pipeline is not itself derived from
  ACLED/GDELT/ICEWS/UCDP, so affiliation with those projects is lower-stakes
  (Tier 1) than in Lane A — there is no source-dependency loop to manage,
  only ordinary domain-expert overlap.
- Exclusion addition: general conflict/political-science scholars who cite
  event datasets but have no methodological contribution to how they are
  built or coded do not qualify.

**Lane C — Research-data curation / open science / reproducibility**
- Minimum expertise: professional or scholarly practice in research-data
  curation, FAIR/data-citation standards, or preregistration/replication
  methodology (Dataverse/Zenodo/DataCite/RDA-adjacent, or a
  political-science-specific transparency initiative such as DA-RT/QDR).
- Independence note: essentially no structural dependency risk on Civica's
  own sources; watch instead for commercial ties to a competing data-archive
  or hosting vendor.
- Exclusion addition: general "open source software" advocates without a
  research-data-specific practice do not qualify — the fit is data curation,
  not software licensing (that is Lane E).

**Lane D — Accessibility**
- Minimum expertise: demonstrated practice auditing data-heavy sites
  specifically — data tables, interactive charts/maps, or filterable
  databases — not generic marketing-site accessibility consulting.
- Independence note: low conflict risk; note only direct commercial
  competition or an existing paid relationship with a comparable reference
  site.
- Exclusion addition: accessibility "thought leadership" speakers with no
  traceable audit/consulting practice do not qualify.

**Lane E — Data rights / database licensing**
- Minimum expertise: scholarly or practitioner record on database rights
  (including EU sui generis database right), open-data licensing (CC0,
  CC-BY, ODbL), or non-commercial-license interpretation.
- Independence note: watch for paid advisory relationships with any of
  Civica's specific non-commercial sources (IPU, Constitute) — that would be
  Tier 2 (disclose and recuse from judging that specific source's terms,
  fine elsewhere).
- Exclusion addition: general IP/copyright generalists with no
  database-rights-specific or open-licensing-specific record do not qualify.
