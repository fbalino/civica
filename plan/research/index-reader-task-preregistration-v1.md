# Index reader-task preregistration

**Protocol:** `index-reader-task-protocol-v1`

## Purpose

This test asks whether the derivative K1 composite helps qualified readers complete real evidence tasks better than a source-native K0 dashboard. It does not ask which presentation readers prefer.

Thirty or more qualified governance researchers, data journalists, civil-society analysts, or public-sector analysts complete a randomized within-participant crossover. Owners, contributors, duplicate participants, people with the answer key, and failed attention checks are excluded under recorded rules.

The six task families measure native-scale direction, cross-country comparison, publisher uncertainty, source tracing, missing-data comprehension, and rejection of unsupported conclusions. Accuracy and time among correct responses are primary. Uncertainty misconceptions, trace completion, unsupported verdicts, and confidence are secondary.

K1 passes bounded derivative utility only if it improves accuracy by at least 10 percentage points or reduces median correct-task time by at least 20%, without losing comprehension, source tracing, uncertainty awareness, or nonclaim accuracy. A tie goes to K0.

## Current state

The protocol and scoring rules are frozen before human responses. Execution cannot begin until IDX-031 supplies the final K0 dashboard fixture and G5 recruits qualified participants. Agent dry runs may find ambiguous prompts or broken instrumentation. They cannot count as evidence of human comprehension, interpretability, utility, or G5 passage.
