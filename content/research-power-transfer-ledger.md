## What the ledger records {#what-it-records}

The Power and Transfer Ledger is a research prototype built from dated institutional facts. Its intended records are current executive tenure, changes in executive control, election-linked alternation, and constitutional term-limit status. Each published row must carry the statements used to derive it.

The present prototype is narrower. Civica can support a set of current-tenure records, but the database does not yet contain the predecessor chains, ended terms, election-to-term links, and coded constitutional histories needed for historical transfer or term-limit claims. Those fields remain “not computable” or “unknown.”

## Executive identity {#executive-identity}

In a cabinet system, the head of government is the working executive. A ceremonial monarch or president is not substituted for that office. In a single-executive system, the record can resolve only when the sourced executive roles point to one person. Dual, unknown, military, clerical, and party-state arrangements require a rule that fits the actual offices. Collective executives remain contested until a separate collective-office rule has passed reliability review.

Duplicate database rows for the same person, office type, and start date are merged for the prototype. Distinct people are never merged because their office titles look similar.

## Electoral transfer and alternation {#electoral-transfer}

An electoral alternation requires a different person and a different governing party or coalition, plus a sourced link showing that a verified election produced the change. A successor taking office after death, resignation, dismissal, coup, hereditary succession, or an internal party replacement does not meet this definition.

The ledger needs the predecessor term and the relevant election record before it can make this claim. Current rows lack that history, so the prototype does not infer alternation from an incumbent’s start date.

## Parties and coalitions {#parties-coalitions}

Party names are not enough to establish continuity. A renamed party or reconfigured coalition counts as the same governing group only when a versioned crosswalk documents the membership and continuity decision. Otherwise the transfer remains contested. Independent candidates and non-party offices retain their sourced status rather than receiving a synthetic party.

## Indirect selection {#indirect-selection}

An indirect election can support a transfer when the constitutionally authorized electoral body selected the executive and the source links that vote to the term. Parliamentary investiture, electoral-college votes, and assembly selection need their own election record. Appointment by a monarch, military council, party organ, or religious authority is recorded under its actual mechanism.

## Interim, collective, and disputed cases {#edge-cases}

Acting, interim, caretaker, and de facto officeholders are recorded as such. They do not establish alternation unless a separate adjudication proves the election link and governing-group change. Rival claimants and disputed elections remain contested. Collective heads of state and rotating executives need member-level dates and a validated rule before the ledger derives a single current state.

## Tenure {#tenure}

Tenure is calendar time from a sourced start date to the release’s fixed as-of date. A missing start date produces no duration. Tenure describes elapsed time in office. It does not say whether the officeholder was elected, whether elections were competitive, or whether the government is democratic.

## Term-limit status {#term-limits}

A term-limit claim needs the constitutional rule for the correct office, its amendment history, the officeholder’s prior completed terms, and an adjudicated count under that rule. The constitutional excerpt and the counted terms must travel with the status. The current prototype has not assembled that evidence, so it reports term-limit status as unknown.

## Missingness and corrections {#missingness}

The ledger does not treat an incomplete history as evidence that no transfer occurred. A record begins where sourced history begins. Missing parties, terms, election links, or constitutional rules remain named gaps. Corrections create a new version and preserve the prior cited record.

## What this does not mean {#nonclaims}

Alternation is neither necessary nor sufficient for democracy. Long tenure does not establish autocracy. Formal term limits do not prove compliance in practice. The ledger does not add these facts into a country score, grade, or rank.

## Validation before publication {#validation}

The frozen validation protocol requires independent coding, a random citation audit, comparison with established historical leader and election datasets, and a prospective freshness period. Agent review can find errors and prepare cases, but it cannot replace the independent coding or expert adjudication required by the protocol. Derived transfer and term-limit states stay out of public country profiles until those gates pass.
